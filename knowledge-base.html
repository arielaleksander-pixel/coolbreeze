3How to read the Dashboard Health page

Every check is green (OK), amber (look into it), red (a problem) or blue (information only). The checks run by themselves a few seconds after the dashboard opens and again every 10 minutes while it stays open. Press Run checks now any time.

⚠️What it cannot see. A web page cannot look inside ServiceTitan, QuickBooks, the automation that saves the files, Windsor.ai’s settings, or a private GitHub repository without a token. For those it shows the effect (an old file, an empty feed) rather than the cause. When something is amber or red, the matching section of this guide says what to check.

The timing rules used for “stale” are: a daily feed turns amber after 2 days and red after 4 days without an update; an access key turns amber 60 days and red 14 days before it expires. These thresholds sit in one place at the top of the Health script and can be changed. To confirm the real schedule of each ServiceTitan and QuickBooks report.

5The #1 risk: the scheduled reports and the automation
🚨The #1 thing that can silently kill the dashboard: every Excel file depends on a scheduled report being sent and an automation saving it to Azure. If the account those reports are sent to, or the account the automation signs in with, has its password changed (or is locked, loses its license, or is forced to sign in again by an IT policy), the files stop updating and the dashboard slowly goes stale with no error on the dashboard itself.
🚫Do not change the password of the report account without a plan. Tell IT and anyone who can reset passwords. After a password change, the automation’s connections usually have to be signed in again before anything moves.
How a typical report reaches the dashboard
  1. ServiceTitan or QuickBooks runs a scheduled report and sends it out as an Excel attachment.
  2. The automation picks up the attachment To confirm (Best Plumber uses Power Automate for this; Cool Breeze may use the same).
  3. It saves the file into the “coolbreeze” storage account with a fixed file name, replacing the previous copy.
  4. The dashboard downloads that file the next time anyone opens it or presses Refresh.

Three links can break the chain: the report being sent, the automation reading it, and the automation saving it to Azure.

What breaks it
  • Password change on the report account (the most common cause).
  • Company IT policy update, forced sign-out, or a new multi-factor requirement on that account.
  • License removed, account disabled, or the mailbox deleted or full.
  • A ServiceTitan or QuickBooks report schedule edited, paused, or sent to a different address.
  • An email rule that moves the reports out of the inbox before the automation sees them.
What to do after a password change or an “action required” alert
  1. Sign in to the automation tool as the report account and reconnect (reauthenticate) every connection it lists.
  2. Open the automations that feed the core files (Jobs, Invoices, Estimates, Purchase Orders, Bills Quickbooks) and check their run history for a green success.
  3. Resend the reports from ServiceTitan and QuickBooks (or run the schedules manually). Reports that arrived while the connection was broken may not be picked up after you fix it.
  4. Confirm in the dashboard. Reload, press Refresh, and check on Dashboard Health that the files show today’s or yesterday’s date.
  5. Note the date and what you fixed.
🩺How you will see it on the Health page: several ServiceTitan files turn amber/red at the same time while the QuickBooks files stay fresh (or the other way round). The page calls this pattern out explicitly. If both go stale together, the automation itself has probably stopped.

7This year, last year, older years: the yearly update
📅One job must be done by a person every year, in early January. The “this year” and “last year” ServiceTitan reports move by themselves on January 1. The “older years” reports are fixed snapshots that someone has to regenerate by hand. If this is skipped, a whole year of history disappears from the dashboard.

Checklist (first week of January, suggested January 2)
  1. In ServiceTitan, open each “older years” scheduled report (Jobs, Invoices).
  2. Change the end date to December 31 of the year before last year (in January 2027: December 31, 2025). Leave the start date at the beginning of history.
  3. Do not change the report name, column layout or the attachment’s file name.
  4. Send the report now so the automation saves it.
  5. In Azure, confirm the file’s Last modified is today and the size grew.
  6. Open the dashboard, press Hard Refresh, and check one number from the added year against ServiceTitan.
⚠️Make sure “older years” does not overlap with “last year”. Jobs and the AR list skip duplicates, but Construction revenue adds the invoice files together, so an overlap would double count it.
8Routine: 30 minutes a week
  1. Open Dashboard Health and read the banner at the top. Anything amber or red is listed under “Needs attention”.
  2. Check the dates. On the Jobs tab, confirm the latest completed job is yesterday or today. Check the “Export files loaded” badge at the top right.
  3. Click through every tab. Look for blank charts, “—” where a number should be, or error messages.
  4. Spot check two numbers. For example, last week’s completed jobs against ServiceTitan, and the QuickBooks Total on Bills Reconciliation against the QuickBooks Bills report.
  5. Work the reconciliation exceptions. On Bills Reconciliation, look at vendors with a discrepancy; on Membership Reconciliation, look at the biggest differences.
  6. Check Memberships → Data Warnings for active plans with a $0 billing total or a negative deferred balance.
  7. Log anything odd with a screenshot and send it to the dashboard owner.

10How to renew a file access key (SAS token)

Do this when a tab shows a 403 error, or before the expiry date. Container-level tokens are easier to maintain than file-level ones: a file-level token only works for one exact file, so renewing means repeating the steps for every file.

  1. Sign in to the Azure Portal and open Storage accounts → coolbreeze.
  2. Go to Containers and open the coolbreeze container.
  3. Choose Shared access tokens (container level, so one token covers every file in it).
  4. Set permission to Read and List only. Set a long expiry date. Start time a few minutes in the past to avoid clock issues.
  5. Click Generate SAS token and URL and copy the Blob SAS token.
  6. Send the new token to whoever maintains the dashboard and ask them to replace it in the dashboard settings. Each file link lives in its tab’s configuration block, so the old token has to be swapped there.
  7. Publish the updated page, reload the dashboard and click through the affected tab to confirm it loads.
11How to publish a change to the dashboard

All page changes are made in one file: Coolbreeze_Accounting_Dashboard.html.

  1. Take a backup copy of the current file first (name it …backup_YYYYMMDD_pre-<what you’re changing>.html).
  2. Make the change.
  3. Open the page and check every tab loads with no errors.
  4. Publish the file to wherever the dashboard is hosted To confirm.
  5. Open Dashboard Health: section 1 confirms the site now serves the new version.
⚠️The page contains data links. The dashboard file has the Azure file links (with read-only tokens) and the Windsor.ai key built in. Anyone who can open the page can view the source and copy them. Keep any repository private, do not post the dashboard address publicly, and consider putting the site behind a sign-in.

13Rules to protect the data
  • Never rename Excel columns in the ServiceTitan or QuickBooks exports. Add new columns at the end if needed.
  • Never rename or move files in Azure. If a file must be replaced, upload it with the same name.
  • Keep the first sheet first. The dashboard reads the first sheet of each workbook.
  • Do not edit data files by hand. Fix the source in ServiceTitan or QuickBooks and resend the report.
  • Bills match on vendor + month + exact amount. A bill entered with a different vendor name or amount in QuickBooks and ServiceTitan shows as a discrepancy, even if it is the same invoice.
  • QuickBooks “Supplies (Shop)” bills are QuickBooks-only by design and are not expected in ServiceTitan.
  • Construction invoices must stay on the “Job Income: Construction Job” account, or they drop out of Construction revenue on the Jobs tab.
  • Never share SAS links or the Windsor.ai key outside the team.

Part 2KPI glossary 64 entries covering every KPI card on the dashboard: the formula, why it matters and how to read it. Type a KPI name or word in the search box (for example ROAS, churn, margin, CAC).
1Jobs Jobs page · completed work, margin and operations · 15 KPIs
Completed revenueJobs › Business performance · 1
Formula
Σ Jobs Subtotal of completed jobs (completion date in range, filters applied) + Construction revenue (job-less progressive-billing invoice lines on the “Job Income:Construction Job” account, same dates)
Why it matters
The top line: what the company actually earned from finished work. It is the base every margin and % on the page is divided by.
How to read it
Compare it with the target line and the prior period. Growth with falling margin % means you are buying revenue; flat revenue with rising margin is healthy. It is not cash collected (see Accounts Receivable) and not booked sales (see Sales).
Gross profit (after burden & marketing)Jobs › Business performance · 1
Formula
Gross margin − labor burden − marketing spend, where gross margin = revenue − all labor (incl. labor not tied to jobs) − materials − other direct costs. Construction revenue counts as 100% margin (it has no job costs in ServiceTitan).
Why it matters
The dollars left after the costs of doing the work and getting the customer. It pays rent, office salaries and profit.
How to read it
Higher is better. If revenue rises but this does not, costs are rising faster than revenue. The line under the card shows the pieces (gross margin − burden − marketing).
Gross margin % (after burden & marketing)Jobs › Business performance · 1
Formula
Gross profit (after burden & marketing) ÷ total revenue × 100 — one aggregate ratio, not an average of job percentages.
Why it matters
The single best read of pricing and cost control: how many cents of every revenue dollar stay in the business.
How to read it
Higher is better. Compare with the business-unit target. A drop usually comes from labor % or materials % — check those cards and the “How margin is built” table.
Completed jobsJobs › Business performance · 1
Formula
Count of jobs with Status = Completed and completion date in range (warranty/recall and presold installs included).
Why it matters
Volume of work delivered; with revenue it gives the average ticket.
How to read it
Read together with revenue: more jobs and flat revenue means smaller tickets. Click the card for the breakdown.
Labor % of revenueJobs › Profitability drivers · 3
Formula
Total labor cost ÷ revenue of completed jobs × 100
Why it matters
Labor is the largest controllable cost in HVAC service. A few points here move gross margin the most.
How to read it
Lower is better. Rising labor % with flat revenue = lower productivity (more hours per job, idle time, overtime) or price increases that lag wage increases.
Materials % of revenueJobs › Profitability drivers · 3
Formula
(Materials + Equipment + PO/Bill costs) ÷ revenue × 100
Why it matters
Shows whether parts and equipment are priced and bought well.
How to read it
Lower is better. A jump often means un-marked-up equipment, supplier price increases or costs posted to the wrong job.
Marketing % of revenueJobs › Profitability drivers · 3
Formula
Marketing spend (Google Ads + QuickBooks Advertising & Marketing expenses, same dates) ÷ revenue of completed jobs × 100
Why it matters
How much of each revenue dollar goes to getting customers.
How to read it
Lower is better, as long as revenue holds. Use it with ROAS and CAC on the Marketing pages to judge whether spend is working.
Warranty / recall costJobs › Profitability drivers · 3
Formula
Σ (revenue − gross margin) on completed jobs whose type is warranty or recall
Why it matters
The cost of callbacks: work done twice that earns nothing.
How to read it
Lower is better. A rising number points to install quality, training or a specific technician — click to see the jobs.
Scheduled todayJobs › Operations
Formula
Open jobs (Scheduled or In Progress) whose Last Appt Date is today
Why it matters
Today’s workload at a glance.
How to read it
A close proxy only: the Jobs file keeps one appointment date per job. Use the ServiceTitan Dispatch Board for exact appointments.
Unassigned jobsJobs › Operations
Formula
Open jobs with no primary technician
Why it matters
Jobs nobody owns are the ones that get missed or run late.
How to read it
Should be close to 0 for today and tomorrow. Work the list from the soonest appointment.
Overdue open jobsJobs › Operations
Formula
Open jobs (Scheduled or In Progress) whose last appointment date is before today
Why it matters
Work that should have been finished or closed out.
How to read it
Should be close to 0. Usually jobs left open in ServiceTitan after the visit — close them so revenue and margin are counted.
Completed, awaiting invoice reviewJobs › Operations
Formula
Completed jobs in range with revenue > $0 and no Invoice # on the job
Why it matters
Finished work that has not been billed is revenue at risk.
How to read it
Should be close to 0. Anything older than a few days needs an invoice.
Unconverted jobsJobs › Operations
Formula
Completed jobs with $0 revenue whose job type is not Courtesy call, Estimate, PM Tune-up, LOT, Warranty or Recall
Why it matters
Visits that produced nothing: unbilled work, a lost sale or missing pricing.
How to read it
Lower is better. Review each one: bill it, or record why it did not sell.
Low-margin jobsJobs › Operations
Formula
Completed revenue jobs whose gross margin % is below the threshold in 🎯 Targets (or the month’s business-unit margin target); jobs with no labor cost are excluded
Why it matters
Finds the individual jobs dragging margin down.
How to read it
Look for patterns — the same job type, salesperson or technician — rather than one-offs.
Incomplete costingJobs › Operations
Formula
Completed revenue jobs with no labor cost posted
Why it matters
Their margin looks better than it really is until labor is loaded.
How to read it
Should be 0 once timesheets are approved. A high count makes every margin figure on the page too optimistic.
2Sales Sales page · booked sales, conversion and pipeline · 9 KPIs
Booked salesSales › Sales performance · 1
Formula
Σ Estimates Subtotal of Sold estimates with Sold On date in range (filters applied)
Why it matters
What the team sold in the period — the leading indicator of revenue that will be completed later.
How to read it
Higher is better. It is not completed revenue (Jobs) and not cash (Accounts Receivable).
Won opportunitiesSales › Sales performance · 1
Formula
Count of distinct Opportunity Numbers with at least one estimate Sold in range
Why it matters
How many customers said yes, regardless of how many estimates each one had.
How to read it
Read with booked sales: fewer wins but the same dollars means bigger tickets.
Opportunity close rateSales › Sales performance · 1
Formula
Won ÷ eligible opportunities, both from the cohort of opportunities CREATED in the range, outcome as of today
Why it matters
The cleanest measure of selling skill: of the chances we created, how many did we win.
How to read it
Higher is better. The current month always looks low at first and rises as opportunities mature — compare full months.
Average sold estimateSales › Sales performance · 1
Formula
Booked sales ÷ number of Sold estimates (Sold On in range)
Why it matters
Ticket size: whether the team sells complete solutions or the minimum.
How to read it
Higher is better, unless close rate falls at the same time.
Sold then canceledSales › Sales performance
Formula
Sales booked in range whose parent job is now Canceled in the Jobs file
Why it matters
Sales that will never become revenue.
How to read it
Lower is better. A rising count can mean over-selling, financing problems or slow scheduling.
Realized gross profit / gross margin %Sales › Sales performance
Formula
Job-level gross margin $ (revenue − job labor − materials) on completed jobs; % = total gross profit ÷ total revenue
Why it matters
Shows whether what was sold turned out profitable once the work was done.
How to read it
Higher is better. This is before idle labor, burden and marketing — the Jobs page shows the fully loaded figure.
Open opportunitiesSales › Pipeline & follow-up
Formula
Distinct opportunities with an open estimate, no sale, status not dismissed/lost/expired/canceled, parent job not canceled, quote > $0
Why it matters
The pipeline still available to close.
How to read it
A shrinking pipeline today means lower booked sales next month.
Modeled open quote valueSales › Pipeline & follow-up
Formula
Σ per open opportunity of its highest (or median, per the setting) quoted estimate — Good/Better/Best options are never added together
Why it matters
An estimate of what the open pipeline is worth.
How to read it
Not guaranteed revenue — multiply mentally by the close rate for a realistic expectation.
Requiring follow-up / Overdue follow-upsSales › Pipeline & follow-up
Formula
Open opportunities whose Follow Up Date is overdue or within the next 7 days / Follow Up Date before today
Why it matters
Who to call next — follow-up is where most lost sales are recovered.
How to read it
Overdue should be close to 0. Opportunities without any follow-up date are counted separately.

Why it matters
The sales result marketing is judged on.
How to read it
Higher is better. Same figure as Booked sales on the Sales page, with Marketing’s filters.
ROAS (return on ad spend)Marketing › Overview · 1
Formula
Total Sales ÷ Marketing Spend
Why it matters
How many dollars of sales each advertising dollar produced.
How to read it
Higher is better. As a rule of thumb, many service businesses aim for 3x or more; compare campaigns in the ROI table.
Potential ROASMarketing › Overview · 1
Formula
Potential Revenue ÷ Marketing Spend, where Potential Revenue = Σ highest quoted estimate per Opportunity in range
Why it matters
What spend could return if every quoted job closed — separates a lead problem from a closing problem.
How to read it
High potential ROAS but low ROAS = leads are good, sales are not closing. Both low = the leads themselves are weak.
Total LeadsMarketing › Overview · 1
Formula
Lead Calls + Web Forms + Manual Calls in range
Why it matters
The demand marketing created; every conversion rate on the page uses it.
How to read it
Higher is better, if Unqualified and Unbooked do not rise with it.
Lead Calls / Web Forms / Manual CallsMarketing › Overview · 1
Formula
Calls with Lead = TRUE / Web Booking submissions that were genuine leads / Jobs with no Call Campaign (excluding Construction and Job Type “z No Job”)
Why it matters
Shows which door customers came through.
How to read it
A high Manual Calls count is a flag to audit attribution: those leads were not tracked to a call or form.
Total OpportunitiesMarketing › Overview · 1
Formula
Total Leads − Unbooked − Cancellations
Why it matters
How many leads became a real chance to sell.
How to read it
Higher is better. A big gap to Total Leads means booking or cancellation problems.
UnbookedMarketing › Overview · 1
Formula
Calls with Call Type = Unbooked + Web Bookings dismissed for a genuine (non-junk) reason
Why it matters
Leads that wanted service but were not booked — paid-for demand lost at the phone.
How to read it
Lower is better. Check the reasons in Call Analysis (price, availability, area).
CancellationsMarketing › Overview · 1
Formula
Leads whose matched job Status is Canceled
Why it matters
Booked work that fell through.
How to read it
Lower is better.
Unqualified LeadsMarketing › Overview · 1
Formula
Calls where Lead = FALSE and Call Type = Excused (wrong number, spam, out of area…)
Why it matters
Traffic that was never sales-ready.
How to read it
A rising count means campaigns attract the wrong audience — review targeting.
Cost per LeadMarketing › Overview · 1
Formula
Marketing Spend ÷ Total Leads
Why it matters
What one inquiry costs.
How to read it
Lower is better (shown red when it rises). Only meaningful together with booking and close rates.
Customer journey: Saw us → Clicked → Contacted us → Booked → Completed → Hired us → SalesMarketing › Overview · journey

All calls in range / calls with Lead = TRUE / calls with Lead = FALSE (filters applied)
Why it matters
Phone volume and how much of it is real demand.
How to read it
A falling lead share means more non-sales calls (existing customers, vendors, spam).
Business Hours / After Hours / Weekday / Weekend callsMarketing › Call Analysis

Call Time 8:00 AM–4:59 PM on weekdays / at or after 5 PM, before 8 AM or on a weekend / Monday–Friday / Saturday–Sunday

Tells you when customers call, for staffing the phones and answering service.
How to read it
A high after-hours share with many unbooked calls suggests missed after-hours demand.

New customersCustomer Acquisition · 1
Formula
Customers in the Customer List whose First Job Completed is in range (Category / Campaign filters applied)
Why it matters
Growth of the customer base.
How to read it
Higher is better. Needs the First Job Completed column in the Customer List export.
Advertising spendCustomer Acquisition · 1
Formula
Same as Marketing Spend, with this page’s filters
Why it matters
The cost side of acquisition.
How to read it
Shown without green/red: more spend is not good or bad by itself.
Customer acquisition cost (CAC)Customer Acquisition · 1
Formula
Advertising spend ÷ new customers
Why it matters
What it costs to win one new customer.
How to read it
Lower is better — but only compare it with what a customer is worth (LTV : CAC).
12-Month Avg LTVCustomer Acquisition · 1 and 5
Formula
Average Customers Lifetime Revenue of customers whose First Job Completed is in the trailing 12 months (independent of the date range)
Why it matters
What a recently acquired customer is worth so far.
How to read it
Higher is better. It grows as customers come back, so recent cohorts look smaller.
LTV : CACCustomer Acquisition · 1
Formula
12-Month Avg LTV ÷ CAC
Why it matters
Whether customers are worth what they cost to acquire.
How to read it
Above 1.0x = paid back in revenue; 3x or more is a common healthy target. Below 1.0x = acquiring customers at a loss (on revenue).
New / Existing customer sales and share from newCustomer Acquisition · 1
Formula
Sold sales in range split by whether the buyer’s first completed job was before the range (existing) or not (new), matched by phone then name; share = new ÷ (new + existing)
Why it matters
Whether growth comes from new customers or from serving existing ones.
How to read it
Shown without green/red — both are healthy; a sudden shift is worth understanding.
Paying customers / Repeat rateCustomer Acquisition · 5
Formula
Share of the 12-month cohort with lifetime revenue > $0 / share with 2+ lifetime completed jobs among cohort customers whose first job is at least 90 days old
Why it matters
Quality of the customers marketing brings in.
How to read it
Higher is better. Low repeat rate on one channel = one-time buyers; consider memberships.
